GEAMS pilot runbook

For the person running the pilot (the "pilot lead"). Print it. Tick the boxes. Keep it next to the phone you will use for the test.

What this document is not. It does not replace legal advice. Items marked [LAWYER] must be checked by a Ghanaian lawyer or the Data Protection Commission before real people use the system. GEAMS is a pilot tool: keep the existing 112 / police / fire / ambulance phone routes running in parallel for the whole pilot. Do not tell civilians that GEAMS replaces them.

2. Setting up the pilot

Week before

  1. HQ owner signs in, creates the pilot station (and any second station) and prints each station's sign-in details.
  2. Each commander signs in once, sets their personal password, and creates their own commander account.
  3. Officers, fire crews and ambulance crews register in their app. The control room approves each one under Crew approvals. Hand each person their quick-start card (quick-start-cards.html). Tell them to change the first password.
  4. Test with staff acting as civilians first (family and friends of staff). Use the real-phone test script.

Day of launch

  1. Check Storage health is green and the latest backup is under 24 hours old.
  2. Station staff sign in 30 minutes before. Confirm the map shows and a test alert from a staff phone arrives.
  3. Announce through your normal channels. Tell civilians what GEAMS can and cannot do (the privacy notice and the first screen say it too).

When someone leaves or loses a phone

  • Officer / fire / ambulance crew member leaves: control room disables the account (Crew approvals). Their sign-in stops working on the next request.
  • Crew member forgot password: control room resets it (Crew accounts → Reset). They are forced to choose a new one.
  • Suspect a stolen phone or leaked passwords: HQ owner → Rotate tokens. Every staff sign-in ends; people sign in again.
  • Civilian got a new phone: they use Restore my account with the recovery code they saved. Without the code, the old alerts stay with the old phone; they can still make new alerts.

4. Incident-day plan

If GEAMS itself has a problem during a real emergency

  1. Use the normal phone routes at once. GEAMS is not the only way in.
  2. Pilot lead checks HQ → Storage health and the server's status page.
  3. If the server is down: restart it. Data is saved every half second to disk and to the database; nothing should be lost.
  4. If the data looks wrong: do not restore over it yet. Take a copy of the broken state first, then follow the restore steps in the deploy checklist.

Lockdown

If you believe the system is being abused (for example, a flood of fake alerts): HQ owner → Lockdown. Staff and the public can read but cannot change anything except HQ and civilians reporting an emergency. Lift it when safe. Every lockdown is logged.

Rollback (stop the pilot)

  1. HQ owner turns on Lockdown with a clear reason.
  2. Announce through normal channels that the pilot is paused and to use 112.
  3. Export what you need (HQ → Export incidents).
  4. Take a final backup. Keep it as long as the law and your retention policy say [LAWYER].
  5. Leave the server up read-only until everyone has been told.

6. Known limits you must tell people about

  • Face matching is a guide to help a human decide, not proof of identity. It can be wrong, especially for dark skin in poor light, for twins, and for older photos.
  • Location is only as good as the phone's GPS and the signal. Indoors it can be off by hundreds of metres.
  • GEAMS does not call the emergency number for the person. If an alert is not acknowledged, they must call.
  • If the phone has no data signal, the app cannot send. SMS fallback only works if you have set up an SMS provider and tested it.
  • The recovery code is the only way to move alerts to a new phone. If lost with the phone, the old alerts cannot be reclaimed.
  • Data is stored on your server. Backups hold old data until they rotate out.
